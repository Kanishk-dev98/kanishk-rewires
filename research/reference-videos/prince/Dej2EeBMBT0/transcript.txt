You've probably seen the Tesla Roadster stick to a ramp upside down, fly off it, and even jump over Tesla's entire lineup. But have you ever wondered how that's even possible? It's actually two systems working together, and the first one is fans under the car that suck the air out from underneath like a vacuum cleaner. Tesla says they pull up to two G's, which is more than the car's own weight, so it stays glued to the ramp. And the second one kicks in the moment it leaves the ramp, because that suction's gone. So tanks of compressed air fire through 10 nozzles on one side to flip it upright, the same trick SpaceX uses to steer its rockets. It's those same thrusters that launched it over the whole lineup, too.
