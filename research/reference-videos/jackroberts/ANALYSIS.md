**Law, confirmed a third time:**
- The CTA converts everywhere. Comment/like ratios run 0.6 to 1.8.
- What varies is **reach**, and the hook topic decides reach.
- Hooks that win: extreme free scale ("280 AI employees"), famous names in a novel combination, and prohibition or "slop" fear.
- Hooks that lose:
  - niche tools ("stop paying a designer" for launch videos)
  - unbelievable claims
  - lists read aloud
  - googleable tools (no reason to comment)

## The format (to be adopted later, at the user's request)

**Three frame states, joined by hard cuts:**
1. **SPLIT** (default, about 45 to 55% of runtime).
   - The top 45 to 50% is a graphic panel on a white vignette, cream, black or a brand colour.
   - The bottom 50 to 55% is the presenter: head centred or slightly left, cap top at the seam, eyes at about 62 to 66% of frame height, cropped to the chest.
   - A black broadcast mic on a boom arm comes in diagonally from the lower right.
   - No divider: the two halves simply meet.
2. **FULL-GRAPHIC** (about 30 to 40%). The graphic fills the frame; the caption drops to 55 to 65% height.
3. **FULL-FACE** (about 15 to 30%).
   - A punch-in on the same camera, 1.3 to 1.5× tighter, with the face filling the frame.
   - Caption at about 70%.

**The rule: facts go to graphics, opinions go to the face.**
- Full-face lands only on:
  - the claim or payoff ("the whole thing is free", "10x your work")
  - "It's called…"
  - list pivots ("the first one is")
  - opinion or contrast lines ("instead of one model auditing its own code")
  - the CTA, always
- Face beats last 0.5 to 3 s and return every 5 to 10 s.
- When the visuals are strong, face share can drop to about 15 to 25%.

**Continuity across layout changes:**
- Graphic objects persist through the cut and only re-lay out. The YouTube player in the split panel grows into the full-frame graphic.
- So the face reads as a strip sliding under one continuous graphic story, not a separate scene.

**Set:**
- A dark room with RGB practicals (a green LED tube on the left; purple, teal or blue rim on the right).
- Warm, soft frontal key light; shallow depth of field (bokeh shelves and plants); a locked-off camera at eye level.
- Wardrobe: cap plus hoodie or tee. A recurring "BASQUIAT" crown hoodie and his own-brand "Glaido" cap work as signature props.
- Energetic delivery with hand gestures, pointing at the lens, and storytelling blinks.
- The dark set against bright graphic panels makes every cut feel like a channel change.

**Cut rhythm:**
- 3.4 to 4.0 cuts per 10 s (average shot 1.3 to 2.7 s), with flash-frame doubles in the busy edits.
- Inside each shot the graphic changes state about every second, on the spoken noun.
- Every reel cuts to full-face for the last 2.5 to 3.5 s.

## Graphics language

- **One mascot with role props:** the pixel Claude crab stands in for "AI / your agent" and carries a code screen, megaphone, scales or hard hat. It also appears in confused poses ("?" marks) and pops in staggered as a crowd.
- **Recreated, simplified UI:** GitHub Star button with count-up and confetti, Claude Code terminal, PLAN.md, Product Hunt #1 card, keyword box, price cards, token list, a 404 player, payment state flips. At most one real screen recording per reel, framed with a glowing blue border and a cursor.
- **A background "world" per subject's brand:** Claude beige, Gemini blue, React Bits purple, Manus blue. Black for logo reveals and contrast beats.
  - The coherent exception is ANIMATE: one cream node-graph canvas with camera pans.
- **Literal metaphor per number or noun:**
  - coins shrinking for cost
  - a list collapsing for tokens
  - a 404 for "can't"
  - a PAID-stamp calendar for subscriptions
  - a melting site and a rejecting crowd for "slop"
  - ✕ stamp and caution tape for "don't"
  - a notification flipping red → green for "cancelled"
  - a conveyor through a glass box for "rebuilt"
  - code typed while its preview builds in sync for "AI writes it as code"
  - a live text edit (Sam → Alex) for "editable"
- **Motion vocabulary:**
  - scale-pop with overshoot and staggered spawns
  - big count-ups held while the number is spoken
  - logo stroke draw-on, then a glow fill
  - dashed connectors travelling between nodes
  - orbit and swap for "A then B, then they swap"
  - blur → sharp with a detection box

## Captions

- 1 to 3 words per chunk that replace each other (no build-up).
- Small white bold sans on a dark translucent rounded pill: on the seam in split, low-middle on graphics, chest height on the face.
- No keyword colour. Big coloured words belong to the graphic, not the caption.
- The only typographic flourish: at the CTA the keyword appears about 2× larger in **white italic serif with curly quotes** ("split", "watch", "brag"). In ANIMATE it is built into a branded end card: black sans "Comment" plus an orange italic-serif keyword, with a confetti burst and a Link pill.

## Script skeleton (110 to 137 words, about 27 to 44 s)

```
Provocative first line, word one at 0.0 s:
  - extreme-scale claim
  - "someone just open-sourced…"
  - "do not let X unless…"
  - "what happens when…"
  - "X can't…"
  - "stop paying X because…"
→ (stack: number + brand names + outcome in the first ~7 s)
→ "It's called NAME" by ~9 s (often on the face)
→ ease: "just drop it in / one install"
→ mechanism in ONE metaphor (employees & hats; ping-pong between two models)
→ one twist line: "even lawyers" / "then they swap roles" / "but it doesn't just…" / "and it's not slop"
→ "So instead of [old way], just [new way]"
→ "Want it? Comment KEYWORD and I'll send you the link/setup."
```

No recap and no outro. The voice is fast (170 to 229 wpm). His best reel was the slowest at 127 wpm, with 0.5 s gaps that land each list card.
