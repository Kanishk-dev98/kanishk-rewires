## Why it's falling (the bear case)
- **Does the AI spend pay off?** OpenAI's annualized revenue was reportedly near $50B versus earlier estimates of $70B. BofA's Sebastian Raedler: consensus hyperscaler capex went from $330B to $1T, and "most major capital cycles of the past have ended in disappointment."
- **Debt-built data centres.** CoreWeave carries about $50B of debt, its bond yields are above 15% ("distress territory"), and its stock fell about 25% in a week.
- **Inflation and rates.** September CPI was 3.7%, markets priced about a 92% chance of a Fed hike, the 10-yr yield was about 5.1%, and oil was above $100.
- **China.** CXMT's memory-chip breakthrough sparked price-war fears, and Korea's KOSPI fell about 21% in 7 sessions.
- **Insiders are warning too.** Broadcom CEO Hock Tan: "A lot of investors have no clue what they are investing in", "almost like they are in a casino". He also said capital "might be the biggest constraint."

## Why it might pass (the bull case)
- **UBS CIO (Oct 14):** "more of a recalibration of expectations than a fundamental break in the AI investment story." UBS points to about 28% expected S&P earnings growth and chip valuations below long-term averages.
- **Jensen Huang (Oct 16, Berlin, to Bloomberg):** "All of this is going to pass, and the reason for that is very simple." (Caveat: he is Nvidia's CEO, so he has an obvious interest.)
- **The fundamentals are still strong.** TSMC Q3 profit rose 68.7% and ASML beat estimates, yet both stocks fell. Nvidia fell least, and SOXX is still up about 64% YTD.
- **Demand still exceeds supply,** according to both Huang and Hock Tan.

## Dot-com framing (use carefully)
In 2000 the Nasdaq peaked at 5,048.62 (Mar 10), crashed about 78% by Oct 2002, and took 15 years to recover. Today's drop is about 16% from a peak that is still well above January levels. Say it **rhymes** with 2000 but **isn't 2000 (yet)**. A big difference is that today's leaders have real profits (Nvidia trades at about 43x earnings with a ~55% net margin). The risk that rhymes is debt-funded build-out.

## Takeaways for founders and employees (not financial advice)
- **Capital is the new constraint.** Hock Tan says money, not chips, may be AI's biggest bottleneck. Expect pickier investors: lenders are already pulling back from AI infrastructure deals (Volta loan, Firmus Grid IPO). Plan runway assuming the next round is harder.
- **Revenue quality beats hype.** The market is punishing spend that lacks returns, so show real customer revenue, not AI-adjacent narrative.
- **If your pay is in equity,** understand that paper wealth tied to AI valuations can swing 20% in two weeks (Intel, Arm, memory stocks). Diversification is a conversation for your own financial adviser.
- **Don't over-read one week.** UBS expects volatility into earnings season, and Nvidia reports Nov 18.

## Caveats and conflicts
- **Market value:** Bloomberg's headline says $1T; the body/summaries say about $1.6T over six days; Reuters says $1.64T over six sessions, "set to" reach $2T on Oct 15; Stocktwits says $2T over eight sessions (unverified); one aggregator says ~$2.3T (unverified). The $1T "in a day" figure is for all tech on Oct 16.
- **Percent decline:** 16% (Investing.com, from Oct 6), 15.7% (TECHi, from closes), and 11.4% for the week only (KuCoin flash). These cover different windows.
- **2008:** Investing.com and the Yahoo live blog say October 2008. TECHi says the last equal-length run was Oct 1-10, 2008, and the last longer one was 9 sessions ending May 2006. Another aggregator mentions a 9-session record in 1996, which is unverified.
- **Oct 19:** There was no Monday data at research time, so the streak status after Oct 16 is unknown. Check the close before publishing.
- **Earlier Bloomberg framing:** On Oct 14, Bloomberg called the 6-day run the "longest since May 2025". That was before the run reached 8 days.
