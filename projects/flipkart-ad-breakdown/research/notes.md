# Flipkart "Ek Size Bada Le Lo" (BBD 2026): research notes

Researched 2026-10-19. Sources are in `sources.json`.

## Key correction to the brief
The ad is not a clothing ad. It borrows the mother's clothing rule ("buy one size bigger so you grow into it") and applies it to **large-screen TVs** during Big Billion Days. The reel should say this clearly, because the twist is the jump from category to category.

## The ad: verified facts
- **Title:** "Ek Size Bada Le Lo" (Hindi for "take one size bigger").
- **YouTube:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HSezX8rIcw0 on the official Flipkart channel, posted as "Mummy's vision: 10/10. Future-proofing our lives since Day 1!"
- **Published:** 2026-10-03 on YouTube. Trade coverage followed on 9 Oct (Medianews4u) and 12 Oct (Campaign Brief Asia).
- **Length:** 80 s (1:20), not 60 s.
- **Agency:** 22feet (part of Omnicom Advertising India). **Director:** Gaurav Uniyal. **Production:** PotliBaba Media House (Campaign Brief Asia credits).
- **Tone, per trade coverage:** humour and nostalgia, amplified across digital platforms.

## Beat-by-beat (only what sources support)

## Breakdown structure for the reel
- **Insight (the truth):** Indian mothers buy clothes a size bigger so children grow into them. Nearly every viewer has lived it.
- **Tension:** buying a big TV feels like an indulgence, and the family will push back on cost. That is the real purchase barrier.
- **Payoff:** the same mother-logic, "buy for the future, size up", now justifies the bigger screen. The family's own wisdom flips into permission.
- **Product link:** the sale price is what makes "one size bigger" affordable. The insight is not decoration. It answers the exact objection ("too expensive, too big") the category faces. Ishan Mehta: "The creative unlock was using that mantra, typically reserved for apparels, for a category like large screen TVs."

## Why it works: frameworks with citable lines
1. **Insight, or "Truth Well Told"** (McCann's motto since 1912): a good ad starts from something true about people, then tells it with craft. Here the truth is a household rule, not a product feature.
2. **Jobs to be done** (Christensen, HBS Working Knowledge, 2011): consumers "essentially 'hire' a product to do that job." The job here is not "watch TV". It is "upgrade without feeling guilty", and the mother's rule supplies the permission.
3. **Nostalgia lowers money pain** (Lasaleta, Sedikides and Vohs, *Journal of Consumer Research*, 2014): in lab experiments, nostalgia weakened the desire for money and raised willingness to pay. That fits a price-sensitive purchase. It is a general finding, not proof about this ad.
4. **Humour plus relatability:** supported only by trade descriptions ("humour and nostalgia"). No academic source was pulled, so present it as an observation.

## Small-business method: find your customer truth
From Christensen's milkshake study (HBS Working Knowledge): the researcher "spent a full day in one of the chain's restaurants", then "interviewed customers who left with milkshake in hand" about what they were hiring it for.
Practical version:
1. Watch real purchases for a day: who buys, when, what else they buy.
2. Ask five or more recent buyers: "What made you buy it that day? What almost stopped you?"
3. Look for the household phrase or habit behind the answer (like "ek size bada le lo").
4. Tie that phrase to the barrier your offer removes.
