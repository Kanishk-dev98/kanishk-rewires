# Brief: Google is writing part of your ads

**Audience:** Indian small-business owners, marketers and founders who run (or will run) Google Search ads. Non-technical.

**Core insight:** Google now adds an AI-written, positive-only review summary inside Search ads in the US, UK, Australia and India. The advertiser cannot edit it and Google shows it only when it predicts the ad will perform better. So your public reviews have quietly become part of your ad copy.

**Tension:** You pay for the ad and write the headline, but a line inside it is written by Google, from what strangers said about you, and you cannot change a word.

**Angle (operator's perspective):** Most people will read this as "a new ad feature". The useful read is that the lever moved: the words your happy customers use now shape your ad, so the job shifts from polishing headlines to getting specific reviews.

**Progression:** surprise (Google writes your ad) → proof (4 markets, India included) → what it is → the pain (no edit, no documented off switch) → twist (positive only, shown only when Google predicts it helps) → meaning (reviews = ad copy) → action (ask for specific reviews).

**Opening image:** a search for "bakery near me", a sponsored ad appears and a line types itself inside it; a cursor reaches for it and it locks.

**Visual metaphor:** one continuous search-results page the camera scrolls down; the illustrative bakery ad card is the hero object throughout. The twist is a lights-down act where the negative reviews fall out of frame.

**Takeaway:** Ask happy customers to say exactly what they loved, because that is what Google writes from.

**Factual guardrails:**
- Only claims from Google's help page "About Review summaries" (support.google.com/google-ads/answer/18760807), confirmed by Search Engine Roundtable, PPC Land and The Keyword.
- Do not say advertisers cannot turn it off; say Google has not said how (the help page gives no opt-out).
- Do not name review platforms (Google does not), do not claim a launch date per market, do not claim the ad labels the line in any particular way.
- The bakery, its domain and its reviews are an illustration, not a real business; say so in the post caption, not with stickers on screen.
- The takeaway is our interpretation, not a Google statement.
