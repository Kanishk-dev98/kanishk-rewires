## 3. s02 · dReel · 5.7 s

**Voiceover:** The medium isn't there to be bought. It's there to make the large look cheap. That's the decoy effect.

**On screen:** decoy / Small / ₹200 / Medium / ₹420 / Large / ₹450 / media/decoy/stock/cinema_people.mp4

**Visual concept:** Friends laughing over popcorn buckets; the Medium tag dims and gets a red DECOY stamp; Large glows.

**Transition:** cut

- Source c8: Original academic definition: Huber, J., Payne, J. W., & Puto, C. (1982). Adding Asymmetrically Dominated Alternatives: Violations of Regularity and the Similarity Hypothesis. Journal of Consumer Research, 9(1), 90-98. doi:10.1086/208899. Finding: adding an option that is worse than one alternative in every respect (but not worse than the other) raises the share of the option that dominates it. This violates 'regularity', the rule that adding options can't increase an existing option's share. (fact) Journal of Consumer Research 9(1) (Huber, Payne, Puto), 1982-06 https://doi.org/10.1086/208899

## 5. s04 · dReel · 11.3 s

**Voiceover:** Use it in your business. Say your gym sells monthly for fifteen hundred, and yearly for twelve thousand. Add a six month plan at ten thousand, and yearly suddenly looks like the obvious deal.

**On screen:** gym / Monthly / ₹1,500 / ₹1,500 / month / 6 months / ₹10,000 / ₹1,667 / month / Yearly / ₹12,000 / ₹1,000 / month / media/flipkart/stock/gym.mp4

**Transition:** cut

- Source c15: How-to, drawn from the literature. (1) The decoy must be clearly worse than your target on every attribute, but not clearly worse than the competing option. It should sit close to the target, e.g. similar price with fewer features. (2) It works best with numeric, easy-to-compare attributes and with buyers whose preferences are unformed. It works poorly with experienced or visual attributes and with expert or frequent buyers. (3) The decoy's exact position matters a lot, so A/B test where you place it rather than copying the Economist numbers. (fact) Palgrave Communications, 2016-11-15 https://www.nature.com/articles/palcomms201682
- Source c16: Ethics and law: the decoy must be a real, purchasable offer at a true price. A fictitious 'was' or reference price is deceptive under FTC rules: if the former price is 'not bona fide but fictitious', the bargain is 'a false one' (16 CFR 233.1). The FTC's 2022 staff report treats interface designs that manipulate purchase decisions (fake scarcity, fake timers) as dark patterns it will pursue. Don't hide fees in the target tier or fake the decoy's features. (caveat) Legal Information Institute, Cornell Law School, current https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/233.1
