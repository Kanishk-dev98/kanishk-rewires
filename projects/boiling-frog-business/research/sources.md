# Source ledger: The boiling frog effect in business (45s 9:16 Instagram reel)

Researched: 2026-10-17

- **c1** (fact, event 1995-10-31 (Fast Company); 2002 (Hutchison reply, published by Gibbons 2007-12-23)): The 'boiling frog' is a fable/metaphor (a frog dropped into hot water jumps out, but one in slowly heated water stays until it dies) used for failing to notice gradual change. Modern biologists say it is not literally true: real frogs become increasingly active and try to escape as water warms; a frog dropped into truly boiling water is scalded rather than leaping out.
  - Fast Company (Issue 01; web date 10.31.95, 'appeared in the November 1995 issue'), "Next Time, What Say We Boil a Consultant", published 1995-10-31: https://www.fastcompany.com/26455/next-time-what-say-we-boil-consultant
  - Savannah River Ecology Laboratory, University of Georgia (read via Wayback snapshot https://web.archive.org/web/20220214032622/http://archive-srel.uga.edu/outreach/ecoviews/ecoview071223.htm), "The Legend of the Boiling Frog Is Just a Legend (Whit Gibbons, Ecoviews)", published 2007-12-23: https://archive-srel.uga.edu/outreach/ecoviews/ecoview071223.htm
  - Wikipedia (secondary; for 19th-century experiment history: Goltz 1869, Heinzmann 1872, Fratscher 1875, Sedgwick 1888), "Boiling frog", published n/a (accessed 2026-10-17): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boiling_frog
  - Note: VERBATIM QUOTES. Prof. Doug Melton, Harvard Biology (Fast Company 1995): "If you put a frog in boiling water, it won't jump out. It will die. If you put it in cold water, it will jump before it gets hot — they don't sit still for you." Dr. George R. Zug, curator of reptiles and amphibians, Smithsonian National Museum of Natural History (same article): "If a frog had a means of getting out, it certainly would get out." (his full quote opens with a profanity). Whit Gibbons (UGA herpetologist, Ecoviews 2007): "With a real frog in real water, my bet is that as soon as it began to get uncomfortable the frog would jump out if it could." Victor Hutchison, Univ. of Oklahoma (quoted by Gibbons): "The legend is entirely incorrect! ... As the temperature of the water is gradually increased, the frog will eventually become more and more active in attempts to escape the heated water. If the container size and opening allow the frog to jump out, it will do so." Nuance: the fable traces to 19th-century German experiments; Heinzmann (1872) reported normal frogs did not try to escape when heated very slowly (<0.2 C/min), Goltz (1869) found intact frogs did try to escape; Sedgwick (1888) attributed the difference to heating rate. So say 'myth/fable', 'biologists say real frogs try to escape' - do NOT say 'science proved frogs always jump'. Note: Gibbons' column is 2007 (Wikipedia citation date); an earlier 2002 Ecoviews version is referenced elsewhere - not verified.
- **c2** (fact, event 1975 (first image December 1975)): In 1975 Kodak engineer Steve (Steven J.) Sasson invented and built the first self-contained (portable) digital camera at an Eastman Kodak lab; the toaster-sized prototype captured 0.01-megapixel black-and-white images to cassette tape.
  - Eastman Kodak Company, "Kodak Milestones", published n/a (accessed 2026-10-17): https://www.kodak.com/en/company/page/milestones
  - IEEE / Engineering and Technology History Wiki (milestone dedicated 2022-04-26), "Milestones: Handheld Digital Camera, 1975", published 2022-04-26: https://ethw.org/Milestones:Handheld_Digital_Camera,_1975
  - National Science & Technology Medals Foundation, "Steven Sasson - National Medal of Technology and Innovation laureate", published n/a (2009 medal): https://nationalmedals.org/laureate/steven-sasson/
  - Note: Kodak milestones 1975: "Kodak invented the world's first digital camera. The prototype was the size of a toaster and captured black-and-white images at a resolution of 10,000 pixels (.01 megapixels)." Kodak 2010 entry: "Retired Kodak researcher Steve Sasson received the National Medal of Technology and Innovation for invention of the digital camera in 1975." IEEE: "Steven Sasson, an engineer at Eastman Kodak, invented and built a self-contained electronic camera"; patent US 4,131,919 'Electronic Still Camera' (Sasson & Gareth Lloyd, granted 1978). Safe on-screen: 'Kodak built the first digital camera in 1975.' Avoid 'Kodak buried it' as a hard fact - widely told (management reportedly saw no market) but that's interpretation; Kodak later did sell digital cameras.
