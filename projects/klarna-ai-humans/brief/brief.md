# Creative brief: Klarna automated the volume, not the trust

**Audience.** Founders, operators, marketers and CX leads who are deciding how far to automate customer-facing work.

**Core insight.** The popular version is "Klarna replaced 700 agents with AI, then regretted it." The record says something more useful: the AI never left, and it still handled about two-thirds of chats. What changed is that Klarna split the work. AI took the routine volume, and humans were repositioned as the quality layer, which the CEO now calls "the VIP treatment".

**Tension.** A celebrated automation win (the equivalent of 700 agents' work, errands down from 11 minutes to under 2) followed by the CEO admitting that cost had dominated and quality had dropped.

**Why the viewer should care.** Every business is about to make the same decision. The expensive mistake is to optimise only for cost and lose trust.

**Emotional and intellectual progression.** Surprise (700) → doubt (humans again?) → evidence (the numbers were real) → admission (lower quality) → reframe (the AI never left) → model (split the work) → takeaway (three rules).

**Opening image.** Oversized type on charcoal: "The work of 700 agents." The number lands in lime on the spoken word.

**Visual metaphor.** One wire splitting into two paths: routine volume to AI, moments that matter to humans. Fine cream strands run under the dark scenes and converge into the K·R monogram at the end.

**Structure.** Common belief → contradiction → evidence → new perspective → practical takeaway.

**Takeaway.** Automate the volume, not the trust. Measure quality, not just cost. Always leave a door to a human.

**Factual guardrails (from research/notes.md).**
- "700" is Klarna's full-time-equivalent claim, not a layoff count. Never say "fired 700".
- Say "hiring humans" or "investing in human support", not "rehiring".
- Never say Klarna abandoned AI.
- Every AI performance number is a company claim and is labelled "Klarna's own figures".
- Quotes are verified through outlets citing Bloomberg (paywalled), and the label says Bloomberg.

**Format.** 9:16, 1080×1920, 30 fps, about 50 s plus the 3 s brand outro. English narration (Edge TTS, Andrew), captions on, original score.
